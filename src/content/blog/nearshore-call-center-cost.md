Most pricing pages for nearshore call centers show a clean hourly rate and call it done. The number looks good — somewhere between $12 and $22 an hour, depending on the country and the tier of service. But that rate alone won't tell you what you'll actually pay each month, or whether the model you're buying fits the volume and complexity of your support operation.

This post breaks down **nearshore call center cost** by model, region, and what's typically (and quietly) excluded. If you're comparing quotes right now, this is where to start.

## The Three Billing Models — and Why They're Not Interchangeable

Nearshore providers generally price support in one of three ways. Each has a different risk/reward profile depending on your volume.

### 1. Hourly / Shared Agent

Agents handle your tickets or calls alongside other clients. You pay for actual time worked.

- **Typical range:** $12–$22/hour, fully loaded
- **Best for:** early-stage companies, low-volume programs, overflow or after-hours coverage
- **Watch for:** minimum hour commitments, ramp-up fees, and tier floors (many providers require 80–120 hours/month minimum even on shared plans)

### 2. Dedicated Seat / Monthly

You get a named agent (or a pod of agents) assigned exclusively to your account, billed monthly regardless of volume fluctuations.

- **Typical range:** $2,200–$3,400/seat/month in LatAm
- **Best for:** mid-market to enterprise programs needing consistency, brand immersion, and deep product knowledge
- **Watch for:** seat minimums (often 3–5 agents), training time that eats the first month's capacity, and what happens when an agent churns

### 3. Custom / Team Build-Out

Larger programs often negotiate a blended rate that includes dedicated leadership, QA, WFM, and sometimes technology stack. Pricing is bespoke — usually starting above 15 agents.

- **Best for:** full outsourced CX departments or complex multi-channel programs
- **Watch for:** long contract terms, SLA penalty structures, and whether the provider owns the infrastructure or subcontracts it

## What Country Actually Costs What

LatAm pricing varies meaningfully by market. Language capability, agent education, and local labor costs all shift the number.

| Country | Typical Hourly Range (Bilingual) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mexico | $14–$22/hr | Largest talent pool, strong border-city English fluency |
| Colombia | $12–$18/hr | Bogotá and Medellín are mature BPO markets |
| Costa Rica | $14–$20/hr | Higher cost, very strong English accent neutrality |
| Ecuador | $11–$17/hr | Accent-neutral Spanish, full U.S. Eastern overlap year-round, high agent retention |
| Dominican Republic | $10–$14/hr | Caribbean nearshore, smaller talent pool |

These are fully loaded rates — meaning agent wages, benefits, supervision, facilities, and provider margin are baked in. They do not include one-time setup fees, which typically run $1,500–$5,000 depending on program complexity.

## The Hidden Costs Quotes Don't Include

A provider's headline rate covers the agent. It usually does not cover:

- **Technology integration:** connecting your helpdesk (Zendesk, Freshdesk, Intercom) to their systems often carries a one-time setup charge or requires professional services hours
- **Training ramp:** the first two to four weeks of a new agent's deployment are typically billed at full rate but deliver 40–60% of steady-state output
- **QA and coaching:** some providers bundle basic QA; dedicated QA analysts cost extra
- **Attrition replacement:** if a dedicated agent leaves, re-hiring and retraining is rarely free — clarify this in the contract
- **Language testing and selection:** providers that rigorously screen for true bilingual fluency (not just conversational English) often charge a premium; those that don't may not disclose the gap until your CSAT reports do

## The 30–45% Savings Figure: What It Actually Reflects

You've probably seen claims that nearshore outsourcing saves 30–45% versus equivalent domestic operations. According to Auxis, a LatAm nearshore consultancy with over two decades of data, that range holds — but it's measured against fully loaded U.S. agent costs, not just wages.

Once you factor in U.S. employer taxes, benefits averaging $6,500–$9,000 per employee annually, recruiting, software, and supervisory overhead, a domestic customer support rep that earns $19–$20/hour lands closer to $28–$38/hour in true cost. The 30–45% savings figure compares that fully loaded number to a nearshore all-in rate — which makes the math work even when nearshore billing looks higher than offshore alternatives.

The part that often gets left out: the savings are real, but they take two to three months to materialize after a program launches. Ramp time is real time, and it costs money.

## When Nearshore Is Not the Right Call

Honest answer: nearshore doesn't win every scenario.

**Offshore (Philippines, India, Eastern Europe) may fit better if:**
- Cost is the primary driver and quality/CSAT is secondary
- Your support is primarily asynchronous (email/ticket) with low complexity
- Your volume is large enough to absorb the higher management overhead offshore programs typically require

**Keeping it in-house may fit better if:**
- Your product is technically complex and agents need constant access to engineering
- You're pre-PMF and support is still teaching the product roadmap
- You have fewer than 3–4 agents and the management overhead of a vendor relationship would exceed the cost savings

The goal isn't to pick nearshore because it's popular. It's to match the model to where your operation actually is.

## What to Ask Before You Sign

If you're gathering quotes right now, these questions separate providers that know their model from those who are just selling seats:

1. What's the minimum monthly commitment — in dollars and in hours or seats?
2. How is training time billed, and what performance standard triggers "steady state"?
3. What's your agent attrition rate, and what does replacement look like contractually?
4. Is bilingual fluency tested at hiring, and can I see your screening rubric?
5. What technology is included, and what triggers additional fees?

Getting these answered in writing, before a contract, removes roughly 80% of the surprises that sour nearshore relationships in the first six months.
