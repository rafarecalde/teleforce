Research from Landbase puts a hard number on a problem every VP of Sales already feels in their gut: **67% of sales are lost because reps fail to qualify leads effectively.** That is not a pipeline problem. It is a filter problem — and it costs you in wasted payroll, missed quota, and burned-out closers who spend their days pitching people who were never going to buy.

A structured **lead qualification** process is the fix. This post walks through the most battle-tested frameworks, shows where each one fits in a modern B2B sales motion, and explains how outsourced SDR teams can do the heavy lifting before a single lead touches your closer.

## Why Qualification Fails in the First Place

The usual suspect is volume. Marketing hands over a list, someone calls it a pipeline, and closers inherit the mess. According to Salesmate's 2026 research, sales reps spend only **28% of their time actually selling** — the rest evaporates into admin, meetings, and chasing leads that never had budget or authority in the first place.

The second suspect is inconsistency. Without a shared qualification standard, one rep's "hot lead" is another rep's time sink. The fix is choosing a framework and running every prospect through it before the handoff — every time.

## The Four Frameworks Worth Knowing

### BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline)

The oldest framework still in circulation, developed at IBM in the 1950s. It asks four questions:

- **Budget** — Does the prospect have funds allocated, or are they window-shopping?
- **Authority** — Is this person a decision-maker, or will you need three more calls to reach one?
- **Need** — Is there a genuine, felt pain that your solution solves?
- **Timeline** — Are they ready to act in the next 30–90 days, or "sometime this year"?

BANT is fast and universally understood. Its weakness: it is supplier-centric. Pushing too hard on budget early in a call can feel interrogative and kill rapport. Use BANT as a mental checklist, not a script.

### CHAMP (Challenges, Authority, Money, Prioritization)

CHAMP flips BANT's opening move. Instead of asking about budget first, it leads with **Challenges** — what pain is the prospect trying to solve? This buyer-first approach opens conversations more naturally, especially in complex B2B sales where the prospect may not have a budget line yet but has a burning problem.

The swap from "Budget" to "Money" and adding "Prioritization" also acknowledges that budget can be created if the pain is real and the project is high enough on the org's priority list. CHAMP is a better fit for consultative sales environments.

### MEDDIC (Metrics, Economic Buyer, Decision Criteria, Decision Process, Identify Pain, Champion)

MEDDIC is the enterprise-grade framework. It was built for complex, multi-stakeholder deals with long sales cycles, and it requires more discovery time upfront. The key additions over BANT are:

- **Metrics** — quantified business impact the prospect expects ("reduce churn by 15%")
- **Economic Buyer** — the single person who controls the budget and signs the check
- **Decision Criteria** — the explicit criteria your deal will be scored against
- **Champion** — an internal advocate who sells on your behalf when you're not in the room

MEDDIC dramatically improves forecast accuracy and win rates on six-figure deals. It is overkill for transactional sales but essential for anything above $25K ACV.

### ANUM (Authority, Need, Urgency, Money)

A leaner variant that puts Authority first because, in practice, talking to the wrong person is the single biggest time drain. If you cannot confirm authority in the first five minutes, the discovery call is at high risk of going nowhere. ANUM is popular with high-velocity inside sales teams where speed-to-decision matters more than deep discovery.

## Matching the Framework to Your Motion

| Sales motion | Best-fit framework |
|---|---|
| High-velocity SMB, <$10K ACV | BANT or ANUM |
| Consultative mid-market | CHAMP |
| Enterprise, multi-stakeholder | MEDDIC |
| Inbound-first, buyer-led | CHAMP or MEDDIC |

No framework works if the person doing the qualifying is distracted by quota pressure, insufficient training, or a lead list that mixes cold prospects with warm responders. That is where the staffing model matters as much as the methodology.

## What to Hand Off (and What to Withhold)

A common mistake: handing off too early because a lead "seems interested." Interest is not qualification. Before a lead becomes a SQL (Sales Qualified Lead), your SDR team should have confirmed:

- At minimum, two of the four BANT criteria (authority + need at the very least)
- A documented pain statement in the prospect's own words
- A confirmed next step the prospect agreed to (not just "they said call back anytime")
- A timeline signal — even a soft one — that separates active evaluation from passive curiosity

Build that checklist into your CRM as a required field before a lead status can flip to SQL. Friction in the handoff process is a feature, not a bug.
