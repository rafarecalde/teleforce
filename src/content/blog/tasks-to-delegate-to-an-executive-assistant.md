If your calendar is full but your priorities aren't moving, the problem usually isn't effort—it's that too much low-value work is still landing on your desk. The fastest fix is knowing exactly which **tasks to delegate to an executive assistant** so you can free your hours for the work only you can do.

Below is a categorized, scannable list. You won't hand off all of it at once—start small and expand—but treat this as the menu. If you're new to handing work off, pair this with our step-by-step guide on [how to delegate to an executive assistant](/blog/how-to-delegate-to-an-executive-assistant).

## Why Delegate at All

Every hour you spend booking travel or chasing a vendor is an hour not spent on strategy, sales, or building. The goal of delegation isn't to look busy-free—it's to concentrate your time on the highest-leverage work and let a capable assistant own the rest. A dedicated EA doesn't just complete tasks; they remove entire categories of friction from your week.

Here's where to point them.

## Inbox & Email

The inbox is where most leaders lose their mornings. An EA can own it end to end:

- Triage and sort incoming mail; flag only what needs you
- Draft replies in your voice for your approval
- Unsubscribe, archive, and kill the noise
- Manage newsletters, receipts, and confirmations
- Route requests to the right person so you never touch them
- Keep the inbox at or near zero on your behalf

## Calendar & Scheduling

Owning the calendar is one of the highest-return handoffs there is:

- Schedule and reschedule meetings without back-and-forth
- Protect deep-work blocks and buffer time
- Coordinate across time zones and multiple parties
- Send agendas, reminders, and prep materials in advance
- Handle cancellations and conflicts before they reach you

## Travel & Logistics

Travel is high-effort, detail-heavy, and perfect to hand off:

- Research and book flights, hotels, and ground transport
- Build detailed itineraries with confirmation numbers
- Manage loyalty programs and points
- Rebook and troubleshoot when plans change mid-trip
- Coordinate visas, dinner reservations, and on-site logistics

## Research & Prep

Give your EA a question and get back a decision-ready brief:

- Vendor, tool, and software comparisons
- Prospect and client background before meetings
- Market and competitor scans
- Summaries of long reports, contracts, or articles
- Options with a recommendation, not just raw links

## CRM & Data

Clean data compounds; messy data costs deals. An EA keeps it tidy:

- Log calls, notes, and next steps after meetings
- Update contact records and deal stages
- De-duplicate and clean stale entries
- Build simple reports and pull lists
- Keep pipeline hygiene so your numbers are trustworthy

## Vendors & Purchasing

Let your assistant be the point of contact so you're not the bottleneck:

- Source quotes and compare vendors
- Place orders and track deliveries
- Manage subscriptions and renewals
- Handle returns, disputes, and follow-ups
- Maintain a vendor and account directory

## Hiring & Recruiting Support

An EA can carry the administrative weight of hiring:

- Post roles and manage inbound applications
- Screen resumes against your criteria
- Coordinate and schedule interviews
- Send follow-ups and status updates to candidates
- Prep onboarding materials for new hires

## Finance & Expenses Admin

Not accounting—the admin around it, which still eats hours:

- Log and categorize expenses
- Prepare expense reports and reimbursements
- Track invoices and send payment reminders
- Organize receipts and documentation for your bookkeeper
- Flag unusual charges and expiring subscriptions

## Personal & Life Admin

The blurry line between work and life is where a dedicated EA shines:

- Book personal appointments and reservations
- Order gifts, flowers, and cards on schedule
- Coordinate household services and repairs
- Manage renewals—licenses, memberships, registrations
- Handle the errands that quietly fragment your focus

## Delegate by Frequency and Judgment

Not sure where to begin? Sequence your handoffs by how often a task recurs and how much judgment it requires. Start in the top-left and work outward.

| | Low judgment | High judgment |
|---|---|---|
| **High frequency** | Start here: inbox triage, scheduling, expense logging | Grow into: inbox managed to zero, calendar owned outright |
| **Low frequency** | Easy wins: travel booking, subscription renewals | Later: vendor negotiation, hiring coordination, research briefs |

The bottom-right quadrant is where your EA ends up after 60–90 days of built trust and solid SOPs—not week one.

## Start Small, Then Compound

You don't need to delegate this entire list tomorrow. Pick the three tasks that drain you most this week and hand those off first. As SOPs form and trust builds, keep pulling more from the list.
