## What It Actually Costs to Build an In-House SDR Team

The salary line is the one everyone sees. The fully loaded cost is the one that stings.

A mid-market U.S. SDR earns a base of roughly $55,000–$70,000. Stack on employer payroll taxes, benefits, commissions, a seat in your CRM, a sequencing tool, data subscriptions, a headset, and your time managing them — and industry benchmarks consistently put the all-in cost at **$125,000–$150,000 per rep per year** (Boomsourcing, 2025; Leads at Scale, 2024).

That figure assumes the rep actually ramps. Most don't ramp in under 90 days. The realistic timeline from offer letter to consistent pipeline contribution is closer to four to five months.

Then there is the turnover problem. Research from **The Bridge Group**, a widely cited B2B sales research firm, puts average SDR tenure at roughly **14 months** with an annual churn rate near **39%**. Run that math: you spend four months ramping someone, get roughly ten months of productive output, then restart the cycle. Each restart costs an estimated $8,000–$15,000 in re-hiring and lost productivity, on top of the base salary already spent.

The compounding effect of ramp + turnover is why in-house SDR programs often underdeliver against their pipeline targets in the first year — even when leadership does everything right.

## What an Outsourced SDR Team Actually Looks Like

The outsourced SDR market has matured significantly. The cheap-offshore-dialers era is largely behind us. Modern outsourced SDR partners embed dedicated reps into your process, use your messaging, work inside your CRM, and hand off meetings the same way an internal rep would.

What you are buying is primarily three things:

**1. Speed to pipeline.** A credible outsourced team can run your first sequences within four to six weeks of kickoff — no hiring cycle, no 90-day ramp, no severance exposure.

**2. Predictable unit economics.** Outsourced SDR retainers run at a predictable monthly rate that comes in well below the fully loaded in-house figure — often a fraction of what a domestic hire costs once salary, benefits, tools, and ramp time are stacked together.

**3. Structural flexibility.** You can right-size the engagement. Need one SDR for a new product launch? Done. Need to scale to three for a seasonal push? No headcount approvals required.

The honest tradeoff: an outsourced team requires a clear ICP, sharp messaging, and a sales leader willing to stay close during onboarding. Handing off a vague brief and expecting magic is a recipe for disappointment on either side of the build-vs-buy ledger.

## The Hidden Variable: Time Horizon

Build-or-buy usually resolves to a time horizon question.

If you are **pre-product-market fit** or testing a new segment, outsourcing is almost always the right call. You learn what works without burning a year of salary on a hire who becomes an experiment.

If you are **past product-market fit** and adding SDRs to a proven motion, the calculation gets closer. In-house reps who stay past the 18-month mark develop institutional knowledge that is hard to replicate. They understand the nuances of your buyer, they recognize familiar names, they know when a deal is real.

The pragmatic answer for most growth-stage B2B companies: **start outsourced, run it as a pipeline lab, then layer in a small in-house team once the playbook is proven.** You get speed-to-pipeline now, and you build institutional knowledge without betting on it before you know what works.

## What to Evaluate in an Outsourced SDR Partner

Not every outsourced SDR offering is equivalent. Before you sign, pressure-test these five areas:

**Dedicated vs. shared reps.** Shared-rep models (one SDR juggling eight clients) produce diluted focus and generic outreach. Look for dedicated seat assignments.

**Messaging ownership.** Who writes the sequences? If the partner hands you a generic template library, the output will be generic. The best providers treat messaging as a collaborative, iterative process.

**CRM integration.** Your reps should work inside your system of record, not a parallel spreadsheet. Handoffs need to be seamless for your AEs.

**Reporting cadence.** Weekly pipeline reviews with activity data, meeting outcomes, and objection logs are table stakes. If a partner can't report at that frequency, they are not managing closely enough.

**Language coverage.** If any portion of your ICP is Spanish-speaking, this is non-negotiable.
