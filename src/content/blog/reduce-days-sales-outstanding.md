# Reducing DSO with Outsourced Accounts-Receivable Support

Every day an invoice goes unpaid, your business is extending an interest-free loan. Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) is the metric that tells you exactly how expensive that generosity is. When you reduce days sales outstanding, you don't just improve a number on a dashboard — you unlock working capital that was already yours to spend.

The challenge is that most AR departments are stretched thin. Follow-up calls fall behind. Aging buckets creep from 30 days to 60 days to 90+ before anyone applies real pressure. By the time a balance lands with a third-party agency, you've already paid a steep price in relationship damage, commission fees, and write-off risk.

Outsourced AR support addresses the problem earlier, more consistently, and at a fraction of the cost of letting balances age.

## What DSO Actually Tells You

DSO measures the average number of days between issuing an invoice and collecting payment. The formula is straightforward: divide outstanding receivables by total credit sales, then multiply by the number of days in the period.

Industry averages vary widely — construction companies routinely sit at 90-plus days due to milestone billing and retainage, while SaaS businesses typically target 30-45 days. But the benchmark that matters most is your own trend line. A DSO that has climbed 15 days over the past two quarters without a corresponding jump in revenue is a warning sign, not a coincidence.

Research from PYMNTS — conducted with AR technology firm YayPay — found that 62% of firms that automated and strengthened their AR processes reported measurable DSO improvement. The firms that didn't automate saw collections cycles stall. The pattern holds whether you're running a technology platform, a healthcare services company, or a B2B distributor: consistent, early outreach is what separates a 35-day DSO from a 65-day one.

## Why AR Follow-Up Breaks Down In-House

In-house AR teams are usually small relative to the volume they handle. When headcount is tight, the team prioritizes the largest open balances. Mid-tier accounts — the ones that collectively represent a significant share of revenue — get inconsistent follow-up. A call might go out on day 20. If there's no answer, the account sits until day 45.

That gap is where DSO inflates.

Compounding the problem: many U.S. companies have substantial customer bases where the primary language is Spanish. When a bilingual agent isn't available for outreach, those accounts receive fewer touchpoints. The result is not necessarily unwillingness to pay — it's a communication gap that delays resolution.

Outsourced AR support solves both problems. A dedicated team handles follow-up on a disciplined schedule, working every aging bucket on cadence, not when capacity allows. Bilingual agents reach Spanish-speaking accounts in the customer's preferred language — reducing friction and accelerating payment decisions.

## The Early-Bucket Advantage

The fastest path to a lower DSO is catching accounts before they become delinquent. Outreach in the 1-to-30-day window, sometimes called pre-delinquent or early-out collections, costs far less than late-stage recovery and recovers cash before customer relationships fray.

When a customer misses a due date by a week, they usually haven't decided not to pay. A friendly reminder — by phone, email, or text — is often enough to resolve the balance. That same account, ignored until day 60, is more likely to dispute the invoice, escalate internally, or require escalation on your side.

[Pre-delinquent outreach](/blog/pre-delinquent-collections) structures this early-stage contact as a natural extension of the customer experience rather than a collections action. The tone is service-oriented. The goal is resolution, not pressure. The effect on DSO is material.

## First-Party vs. Third-Party: The DSO Relationship

There's an important structural distinction in how outsourced AR can be deployed. First-party collections means outreach happens in your company's name — your agents (or outsourced agents acting as your agents) contact customers directly. Third-party collections means turning the balance over to an external agency acting in its own name.

Both have a role, but for DSO reduction, first-party early-stage outreach is the lever that matters most. [Understanding the difference between first-party and third-party collections](/blog/first-party-vs-third-party-collections) helps finance and operations leaders make better decisions about when to escalate — and how to preserve the customer relationship while they do.

The practical implication: if your goal is to reduce days sales outstanding without burning customer goodwill, you want first-party, relationship-preserving contact in the 1-to-60-day window. Third-party agencies are a downstream tool for late-stage or charged-off balances, not a substitute for early follow-up.

From a compliance standpoint, first-party collections generally fall outside the scope of the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA), though TCPA regulations still govern how and when agents can call or text. This is directional context, not legal advice — your counsel should confirm the specifics for your industry and customer base.

## What Outsourced AR Support Looks Like in Practice

A well-structured outsourced AR engagement does several things that an understaffed in-house team typically can't:

**Consistent cadence across all aging buckets.** Every account in the 0-30, 31-60, and 60-90 day windows gets outreach on schedule — not when someone has capacity.

**Bilingual outreach for Spanish-speaking accounts.** Native-Spanish agents reach customers in their preferred language, reducing the communication delays that inflate DSO for bilingual customer bases.

**Clear escalation protocols.** Accounts that don't resolve in the early window are escalated on a defined timeline, not left to sit until quarter-end.

**Reporting that connects AR activity to DSO movement.** You can see which buckets are improving, which accounts are repeat latecomers, and where process changes would have the most impact.

## Connecting AR Outsourcing to Cash Flow

The cash flow math on DSO improvement is straightforward. If your company carries $2 million in monthly revenue and reduces DSO by 10 days, you unlock roughly $667,000 in cash that would otherwise be sitting in open receivables. That's working capital available for operations, payroll, or investment — without new credit.

Outsourced AR support is not purely a cost-reduction play. It's a revenue-recovery and cash-flow optimization play. The cost of the outsourced team is typically a fraction of the cash recovered through more consistent follow-up. The payback is measurable and relatively fast.
